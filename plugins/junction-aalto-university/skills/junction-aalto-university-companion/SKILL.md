## Existing additional support workflows

### 1. Map a research question

Input: research interest. Deliver: concept map and source search plan.

Define the population, interaction and outcome. Find publication or project pages when retrieval is available, cite actual records, and leave unsupported findings blank.

Example request: “Help me find Aalto research relevant to speech interfaces for older adults, and separate findings from open questions.”

### 2. Design a small experiment

Input: idea and intended users. Deliver: hypothesis, measures and study outline.

Separate usability observations from health outcomes. Include consent, accessible participation, a stopping rule and a practical success measure.

Example request: “Outline a small usability study for a voice interface, with a measurable hypothesis and consent considerations.”

### 3. Read a supplied paper critically

Input: paper excerpt or official publication link. Deliver: claim-evidence-limitations review.

Identify study setting, sample and method only when stated. Avoid generalizing beyond evidence. Cite the excerpt and clearly distinguish your proposed next experiment.

Example request: “Review this Aalto paper excerpt: what was measured, what was not, and what should I test next?”
